# Comparing the three concepts

| Criterion | A · Quiet Future Gallery | B · Kinetic Index | C · Digital Atelier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Identity and originality | The live workspace makes this recognisably Ruizhe's. The editorial work sequence is familiar and relies on scale and image treatment for distinction. | The index makes browsing feel active and gives the dark direction a clear purpose. The functionality is stronger than the personal signature. | The most personal wordmark and strongest material contrast. The oversized serif gives it a different sensibility from a typical technology portfolio. |
| Typography | Strong hierarchy and flexible line breaks. Body and navigation remain quiet. | Strongest label-to-stage relationship. The more compressed headlines need deliberate wrapping. | The display serif brings character at large sizes. Its thin strokes are unsuitable for controls, so Geist carries those. |
| Projects | Both projects remain visible and independently linkable. Tall features take more scrolling. | Fast comparison with one large stage. Only one detailed preview is visible at a time. | The digital and physical pieces read as separate exhibits. The staggered layout needs careful sizing as the collection grows. |
| Motion and navigation | Simple discovery via whitespace and a linked feature. The workspace itself remains the richer interaction. | Clearest motion feedback. Hover auditions a work, while click, keyboard or tap holds the selection. The stage link is a separate step. | The signature and crop are the principal gestures. Large media makes it easy to see what has a destination. |
| Responsive and accessible | Media and copy stack at phone widths and link targets stay large. The real scene is the main mobile performance cost. | On mobile, the list stays above the preview and taps replace hover. Named buttons and pressed state document selection. | Mobile preserves the material hierarchy with a large FixLens exhibit and a narrower Sippy exhibit. It loses some of the desktop juxtaposition. |
| Complexity | Medium. Reuse the single live canvas and its established object navigation. | Medium. Selection, hover, keyboard focus and the preview destination must stay in sync. No WebGL in the study. | Low to medium. Mostly CSS and two authentic media compositions. Project-specific image placement takes manual art direction. |

**Recommendation.** Take A into Phase 5B. It makes the personal identity legible without competing with the workspace, and it lets each project tell its own story with one clear link. B is the stronger option if the portfolio grows into a larger catalogue. C is the right choice if a more expressive studio identity matters more than the clean digital-product emphasis.
